# NYC mayor budget math

**Goal:** Test whether the mayor's headline affordability pledges add up — campaign cost estimates vs. campaign revenue proposals, scaled against the enacted FY2027 city budget.

**Macro steps:**
1. Collected published cost estimates for the six signature pledges (campaign's own figures as the low case, independent analyst ranges as the high case).
2. Collected the campaign's three revenue proposals with the same sourcing.
3. Compared total pledged cost range against pledged revenue (coverage ratio), and pledges as a share of the $125.8B adopted FY2027 budget.
4. Plotted costs vs. revenue and wrote the pledge table to `results.csv`; split each pledge into operating vs. capital, ran an operating-only scenario against Albany-dependent vs. city-controlled revenue (`scenarios.csv`, second chart).
5. Tested Albany partial approval (both taxes / corp only / income only / neither) against operating-only costs (`sensitivity.csv`, third chart).
6. Tested childcare across its full published cost range ($2.5B–$12.7B) with other operating pledges held at campaign figures, and solved for the break-even childcare cost (`childcare_sensitivity.csv`, fourth chart).
7. Tested pledged revenue at 100/75/50/25% realization against operating-only and total costs, and solved for the break-even realization rate (`revenue_sensitivity.csv`, fifth chart).
8. Crossed the two revenue risks into a joint grid: each Albany outcome (both / corp only / income only / neither) at each realization rate, with a break-even realization per outcome (`joint_sensitivity.csv`, sixth chart, heatmap).
9. Crossed the biggest cost risk against the revenue risk: each childcare cost case ($2.5B / $5.9B / $7.6B / $12.7B) against each Albany outcome, with a break-even childcare cost per outcome (`childcare_albany.csv`, seventh chart, heatmap).

**Versions:** v1 — initial analysis. v2 — operating-only scenario + Albany-dependency revenue cut (new `scenarios.csv`, `results_operating.png`). v3 — Albany partial-approval sensitivity + childcare swing check (new `sensitivity.csv`, `results_sensitivity.png`). v4 — childcare cost sensitivity + break-even childcare cost (new `childcare_sensitivity.csv`, `results_childcare.png`). v5 — revenue realization sensitivity + break-even realization rate (new `revenue_sensitivity.csv`, `results_revenue.png`). v6 — joint Albany × realization sensitivity: 16-combination grid + heatmap (new `joint_sensitivity.csv`, `results_joint.png`) and the repo's first test suite (`test_budget_math.py`, 11 tests). v7 — childcare × Albany sensitivity: 16-combination grid + heatmap (new `childcare_albany.csv`, `results_childcare_albany.png`), break-even childcare cost per Albany outcome, test suite extended to 18 tests.

**Short results:** On the campaign's own figures, the six pledges total ~$17.8B/yr against $10B/yr of pledged revenue — a $7.8B shortfall that widens to $14.7B at the top of published analyst ranges. Strip out the $100B/10-yr housing capital plan (capital, not operating), and the operating pledges (~$7.8B) are covered 129% by pledged revenue on campaign figures — but 90% of that revenue ($9B of $10B) needs Albany approval, and the only city-controlled line (fines/waste, $1B) covers just 13% of operating costs. Partial Albany approval does not close it either: corp tax only ($6B total) covers 77% of campaign-figure operating costs, income tax only ($5B) covers 64%, and childcare alone drives the uncertainty — its $6.8B low-to-high swing is 99% of the operating cost range. Across childcare's full published range, total operating costs run $4.4B–$14.6B: full pledged revenue covers 229% at the $2.5B analyst low but only 69% at the $12.7B high, and the break-even childcare cost is $8.14B — above that, even the full $10B pledged revenue cannot cover the operating pledges. Revenue has no such cushion: 77.6% of the pledged $10B must actually materialize just to cover operating costs at campaign figures, so at 75% realization ($7.5B) the operating surplus already flips to a $0.26B gap, and analyst-high operating costs (146.6%) or all pledges (177.6%) would need more than the full pledged amount. The two revenue risks compound: across all 16 Albany-outcome × realization combinations, only one (both taxes approved and collected in full, 128.9%) covers even campaign-figure operating costs — every partial-approval outcome would need more than 100% realization to break even (corp-only 129.3%, income-only 155.2%, neither 776.0%), and the joint downside (neither tax, 25% realized) collects $0.25B against $7.8B of operating costs (3.2%). Crossing cost against revenue is just as tight: only 5 of 16 childcare-case × Albany-outcome combinations cover operating costs — partial Albany wins (corp-only break-even childcare $4.14B, income-only $3.14B) cover only at the $2.5B analyst-low childcare cost, and with neither tax approved the break-even childcare cost is negative (-$0.86B), because city-controlled revenue ($1B) cannot cover even the $1.86B of non-childcare operating pledges. The enacted FY2027 budget closed its $5.4B gap with savings + state aid + pension restructuring, not the pledged tax hikes.
